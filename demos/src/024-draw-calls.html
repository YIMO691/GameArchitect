Draw Call Lab

Two ways the Coral Garden gets slow. Draw calls: every slip the render thread writes for the GPU costs CPU time. Overdraw: every see-through pixel the GPU paints again costs GPU time. Pick a mode, change one thing, and watch the bars against their budgets.

    Lesson 025 · The Big Catch splash: overdraw
    
    
      
  
      

  
      
    
      What this shows
      
    
      
      
      A draw call is CPU paperwork
      Each slip costs the render thread about 10 µs, and each material switch 20 µs more, however small the mesh. "Free on the GPU" can still be 42.5 ms on the CPU.
      
      
      Share first, then merge
      renderer.material made 1,200 copies. Sharing the materials cut the switches from 1,250 to 56. Only then can instancing merge 1,200 fish into 6 draws.
      
      
      Every merge has a price
      Static batching saves 2.6 ms for 18 MB. Instancing costs 1 µs per fish and moves culling to you. Neither removes a single triangle: at 4,000 fish the GPU is the limit again.
      
    
      
    
      Lesson 025 · Overdraw
      
    
      
      
      Pixels, not particles
      The 10 foam puffs at the lens are 4% of the particles and 69% of the painted pixels. Halving 200 bubbles saves 1.6 ms; capping 10 puffs saves much more.
      
      
      See-through layers are paid in full
      Opaque pixels can be skipped by the depth test. Every see-through layer runs the shader and does a read-mix-write: here 1 ms of pixel work and 4 ms of memory work per screen.
      
      
      The fill budget is shared
      Before the splash, the Coral Garden has room for 0.5 screens. Fading the murk during the close-up frees 2.5, so a 2.0-screen splash fits at 27 ms.
      
    
      
    
      This lab is a simulation, not a measurement. Costs come from Lesson 024: render thread = 2 ms + 0.01 ms per draw call + 0.02 ms per material switch + 0.001 ms per instanced fish; main thread 14 ms; GPU = 3 ms + the larger of the pixel load (24 ms) and the vertex load (6 ms + 3 ms per 400 fish); the game caps at 30 fps. Real per-draw costs depend on the graphics API, the driver and the engine (Vulkan and Metal are cheaper per draw than OpenGL ES), and real engines also spend main-thread time on culling and sorting each object.
      
    
      The Overdraw mode is also a simulation, using Lesson 025's numbers: 1 screen = 1600 × 720 = 1,152,000 painted pixels; each see-through screen costs 1 ms in the pixel station and 4 ms in the output & memory station (2 ms with the 4-byte format); scene loads before see-through layers: vertex 15, raster 5.3, pixel 22, memory 15 ms; GPU = 3 ms + the slowest station. The heatmap is a sketch of the same 240 particles; its colours are not measured. On real phone GPUs, blending happens in on-chip tile memory and costs differ per vendor.
