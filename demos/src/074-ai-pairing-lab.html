Lesson 074: directing AI coding tools

    What this shows

    It knows code, not your code

    An assistant follows rules it's given. Without the architecture, budgets and existing utilities, it writes plausible code that ignores all three.

    Small steps you can play

    An interface first, then one behaviour at a time, each run in the game. Bugs show up in the step that made them.

    Your checks, your review

    Its own tests check what it meant to write. Your performance test, architecture lint and five-question review check what the game needs.

    This lab is a model, not a measurement. It uses Lesson 074's numbers: 7 problems. Five are architectural (12 hours each to fix after merge): allocation per update, no AI LOD, duplicated utilities, the client deciding catches, and input read directly. Two are bugs (6 hours each): fish slip through the seabed below 200 m, and fish keep moving while paused. An architecture brief (1 hour, reused) prevents the LOD, authority and input problems; small steps (+1 hour) catch both bugs; our checks catch the allocation, authority and input problems; a five-question review (3 hours for the whole feature, 2 in small steps; a skim is 0.5) catches all five architectural ones. A one-line prompt costs 0.2 hours. Fish AI: 0.4 ms with LOD, 2.4 ms without; allocation adds GC spikes.
