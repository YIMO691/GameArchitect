Lesson 019: data-driven design

      
    
    
    
  
    

  
    
    
    What this shows
    
    
    
      
    Code says how, data says how much
    With numbers in code, each tweak waits for a programmer and a compile: 899 s. In data files it's 100 s; reloaded live, 15 s. 120 tweaks drop from 30 hours to 30 minutes.
    
      
    Read, don't copy; check everything
    A value copied at spawn never sees a live change. Data with no schema breaks quietly: one extra "e" stops every gull diving. A validator and stable IDs catch all three mistakes on save.
    
      
    Text for people, binary for the phone
    Parsing 3.2 MB of JSON costs 1,900 ms and 41 MB of garbage at start-up. A bake step makes one 0.6 MB file that loads in 40 ms. Typed structs cut tuning reads from 0.27 ms to 0.006 ms a tick.
    
    
    
    
    This lab is a simulation, not a measurement. The times are the lesson's numbers: 720 s waiting for a programmer, 60 s to edit, 29 s to compile the Simulation (Lesson 018), 90 s to play back to the Frenzy, 10 s to edit a file and 5 s to see a live change. The phone numbers are the lesson's approximate profiler capture. The reef animation only shows the effect of each value; it is not Sky Hopper's real simulation.
