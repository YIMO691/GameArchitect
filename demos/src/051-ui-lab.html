Lesson 051: UI architecture

    What this shows

    One source of truth

    When three panels keep three copies of the coins, they will disagree. Keep the number in one model and let every panel read or bind to it.

    Update what changed

    A coin change should touch one label, not rebuild 400 rows. Bindings turn "something changed" into exactly the work needed.

    Retained or immediate

    Retained UI keeps objects and updates them; immediate UI redraws from the data every frame. Both are fine if they only touch what's visible.

    This lab is a simulation, not a measurement. It uses Lesson 051's numbers: 400 items, 14 visible rows; building a row 0.035 ms and 0.3 KB of garbage; the three panels 0.2 ms to rebuild; updating a label 0.02 ms; drawing a row in immediate mode 0.01 ms plus 0.1 ms for the panels; coins change 4 times a second at 60 frames a second; 340 "wrong coins" reports a day while the panels keep copies.
