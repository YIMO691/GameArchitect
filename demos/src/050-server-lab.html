Lesson 050: rooms, shards, zones

    What this shows

    Crowds cost N × N

    Every player must hear about every player they can see. Ten times the players in one space is a hundred times the work.

    Splitting has a social price

    Rooms and shards are cheap and simple, but they decide who can meet. Zones keep one world, and move the problem to the busiest place.

    Players don't spread out

    At a festival, 60% stand in front of the stage. Plan for the busiest zone, not the average one: send each player only what is near, and split hot zones.

    This lab is a simulation, not a measurement. It uses Lesson 050's numbers: 2,000 players; a tick costs 1 µs for each pair "player A must hear about player B", with a 50 ms budget (20 ticks a second); rooms of 50 run 4 to a process; 15% of friend groups land in different rooms or shards because one is full; at the stage zone 1,200 players gather, the other 800 spread over 9 zones (8 × 90 and 1 × 80); busy zones split into processes of 300.
