Lesson 016: flags, state machines, hierarchical state machines, behavior trees

      
    
    
    
  
    

  
    
    
    What this shows
    
    
    
      
    Flags allow impossible gulls
    5 booleans make 32 combinations, and only 6 mean something. One missed "isDiving = false" left 46 of 1,000 gulls hovering forever. A state machine can only be in one state.
    
      
    Group the interrupts
    A flat state machine needs an arrow from every state to every interrupt: 27 arrows, and one forgotten arrow means 120 gulls fly through the storm. A hierarchical one writes each interrupt once: 13 arrows.
    
      
    Trees decide, but cost more
    A behavior tree turns priorities into the order of its branches, so Steal is 1 new branch. Walking it from the root with world searches inside costs 1.21 ms. A blackboard and resuming the running action bring it to 0.61 ms.
    
    
    
    
    This lab is a simulation, not a measurement. Costs come from Lesson 016 and are approximate phone numbers: brain per gull 4 ns (flags), 6 ns (FSM), 12 ns (HFSM); a tree node 20 ns; a world search inside a condition 300 ns; one batched sensing pass 150 ns per gull; a tree from the root visits 14 nodes on average (10 without storm and stun) and about 2 world searches; a resumed gull visits 4 nodes (2 without storm and stun) and 1 search; 75% of gulls are in the middle of an action. Swim, Fly, Walk and events stay at 0.33 ms. Stuck and wrong gulls (4.6% with flags, 12% carrying when the storm hits) come from the lesson's playtests. The 40 sample gulls are drawn from a simple timed script, not a real AI.
