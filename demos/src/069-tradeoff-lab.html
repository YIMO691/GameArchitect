Lesson 069: evaluating trade-offs

    What this shows

    One number hides the rest

    Picking the cheapest option ignores the one criterion that can't fail: tournament end. Must-haves are a filter, not a score.

    Equal scores are a tie in disguise

    Adding up 1–5 scores treats "cheaper" and "ships two months sooner" as equally important. Weights from the team's goals make the difference visible.

    Check what would change your mind

    Try the weights you're unsure about. If the winner flips, make it robust (here, an interface in front of the managed product) or write down the condition to revisit.

    This lab is a model, not a measurement. It uses Lesson 069's scores (1–5) for each option: game servers cost $1,200 a month, ship in 6 weeks, handle 20,000 entries a minute; a new service costs $2,000, ships in 12 weeks, scales; the managed product costs $3,500, ships in 3 weeks (4 with our interface in front), scales, and locks us in (flexibility 2, or 4 with the interface). Weights for "ship this summer": cost 1, time 3, operations 2, flexibility 2, team skills 1; "flexibility first": cost 1, time 1, operations 2, flexibility 5, skills 1. When peak load isn't a must-have, it counts with weight 1.
