Lesson 067: technical design documents

    What this shows

    A decision not written is made three times

    Each team fills the gap with its own guess. The guesses only meet at integration, where every mismatch is rework.

    Longer isn't clearer

    A 40-page spec has every answer, but readers find only half, and its review takes two weeks. Short, decision-first documents get read.

    Owners catch the wrong guesses

    The author can't know every system. A one-hour review by each owner turns wrong interface guesses into 2-hour fixes on paper instead of 12-hour fixes in code.

    This lab is a model, not a measurement. It uses Lesson 067's numbers: 12 decisions (4 about the feature, 3 goals and non-goals, 5 interfaces and numbers). An undecided or unread decision becomes a surprise at integration costing 12 hours of rework. Three interface decisions the author writes alone are wrong; owners' review fixes each for 2 hours. The 5-page doc takes 6 hours to write and 4 hours (one meeting, 1 day) to review; the 40-page spec takes 32 hours and 4 days to write, 16 hours and 10 days to review, and its readers find only 6 of its 12 decisions.
