Cache Lab

A real 500,000-particle pool. Lesson 004: after 3 seconds of play the update gets several times slower; fix it with swap-and-pop. Lesson 005: 64-byte particles waste most of every cache line; split them into arrays and watch the bytes drop from 128 MB to 22 MB. Lesson 006: mix rain and blood at random and watch half the CPU's guesses go wrong; fix it with branchless code, SIMD, and splitting by kind.

  • Cache lines and next-door reads
  • Free list vs swap-and-pop
  • AoS vs SoA: loaded vs used bytes
  • Branch guesses, branchless code and SIMD lanes
  • Lesson 010 challenge: predict, then check

Jobs Lab

Lesson 008: the storm's 2,000,000 particles on 1 to 8 threads. Eight threads give only 2×: watch each thread's timeline fill with lock waiting and false sharing, then fix both and hit Amdahl's limit. Lesson 009: the whole frame as jobs. Complete() at once keeps 8 cores 19% busy; a task graph with batches takes the frame from 13.41 to 5.64 ms.

  • Threads vs cores and Amdahl's law
  • Lock contention vs per-thread lists
  • False sharing: 8 counters, one cache line
  • Jobs, dependencies and the critical path
  • How big a job should be
  • Lesson 010 challenge: predict, then check

Tuning Lab

Mia tunes the Feeding Frenzy. Apply a change with numbers in code (899 s), in data files (100 s) or with live reload (15 s), and watch 120 tweaks drop from 30 hours to 30 minutes. Break the data with a typo, turn on the validator, then bake it so the phone loads it in 40 ms instead of 1,900.   • Schemas, validators and stable IDs
  • Source data vs baked runtime data
  • String lookups vs typed structs
  • Scripting layers, hot reload and its traps

Draw Call Lab

The Reef's 1,200 fish were free on the GPU, but one line, renderer.material, made 1,200 material copies: 1,550 draw calls, 1,250 switches and a 42.5 ms render thread. Share the materials (18.62 ms), try static batching (+18 MB), then instance the fish (7.88 ms). New: the Overdraw mode. The Big Catch splash paints 8.0 screens of see-through pixels and takes the GPU to 58 ms. See it as a heatmap, then cut it with cutout shapes, a size cap and a murk fade, down to 27 ms. New: the Culling mode. Lantern Harbor sends 3,120 objects nobody can see: GPU 45.2 ms. See them in red on a map, then add frustum culling for the props, occlusion for the street, and HLOD for the lighthouse view, and compare both cameras. New: the LOD mode. From the ferry, 500 houses and 3,000 pines send 3,360,000 vertices: GPU 45 ms. See the red "too many vertices for the pixels" map, then add house LODs, impostor cards for the far pines, cross-fades, and an LOD bias for the low-end phone.

  • Draw calls and material switches as CPU paperwork
  • Material copies vs shared materials + property blocks
  • Static batching and GPU instancing, and their prices
  • Overdraw heatmap: pixels, not particles
  • Cutout shapes, size caps and a fill budget in screens
  • Frustum, occlusion and HLOD culling, and their own costs
  • Checking every camera, not one
  • LOD levels, pixels per vertex and impostors
  • Cross-fades and LOD bias per phone

Shader Lab

The Reef's all-in-one ReefLit shader computes all 10 features on every pixel: 35 cycles instead of 20, and the GPU jumps from 27 to 36 ms. Turn the features into 12 multi_compile keywords and the GPU is fine, but 4,096 variants take 41 minutes to build. Switch to shader_feature (24 variants, 14.4 s), then fix the night glow that went missing with a uniform branch: 27.84 ms.

  • Shader cost in cycles per pixel
  • Why "× 0" is not free
  • Variant explosion: 2 to the power of keywords
  • multi_compile vs shader_feature, and missing variants
  • Uniform vs divergent branches

How labs grow with the course

Not every lesson needs a new lab. When a later lesson goes deeper into the same idea, it adds a new mode and new steps to an existing lab. That keeps the labs small and lets you compare old and new ideas side by side.
