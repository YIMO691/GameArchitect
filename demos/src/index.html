Cache Lab

A real 500,000-particle pool. Lesson 004: after 3 seconds of play the update gets several times slower; fix it with swap-and-pop. Lesson 005: 64-byte particles waste most of every cache line; split them into arrays and watch the bytes drop from 128 MB to 22 MB. Lesson 006: mix rain and blood at random and watch half the CPU's guesses go wrong; fix it with branchless code, SIMD, and splitting by kind.

  • Cache lines and next-door reads
  • Free list vs swap-and-pop
  • AoS vs SoA: loaded vs used bytes
  • Branch guesses, branchless code and SIMD lanes
  • Lesson 010 challenge: predict, then check

Jobs Lab

Lesson 008: the storm's 2,000,000 particles on 1 to 8 threads. Eight threads give only 2×: watch each thread's timeline fill with lock waiting and false sharing, then fix both and hit Amdahl's limit. Lesson 009: the whole frame as jobs. Complete() at once keeps 8 cores 19% busy; a task graph with batches takes the frame from 13.41 to 5.64 ms.

  • Threads vs cores and Amdahl's law
  • Lock contention vs per-thread lists
  • False sharing: 8 counters, one cache line
  • Jobs, dependencies and the critical path
  • How big a job should be
  • Lesson 010 challenge: predict, then check

Game Loop Lab

One jump on a 30 fps phone, a 60 Hz PC and a 144 Hz PC. With a variable step the jump peaks at 1.500, 1.583 and 1.632 m, and an autosave hitch drops the player through the floor. Switch to a fixed step with an accumulator, then fix the judder and the spiral of death.

  • Variable vs fixed timestep
  • Tunneling: one 0.25 s step skips the floor
  • The accumulator and interpolation
  • Spiral of death and the step cap

ECS Lab

The Reef: 10,000 fish, birds, crabs and gulls on a 30 fps phone. Store them as a fat base class (1.90 ms per step), as components (2.75 ms) or in ECS (0.11 ms), and watch how the CPU walks through memory. Lesson 013: a storm of status effects costs 1.31 ms on archetype tables, 0.54 ms on sparse sets and 0.21 ms on a hybrid.

  • Class tree vs components vs ECS
  • Fat base class: 320 bytes per critter
  • Packed columns and systems
  • Structural changes: tag vs field
  • Archetype tables vs sparse sets vs hybrid

Events Lab

The Feeding Frenzy: 600 catches per step, and five systems want to know about each one. Tell them with direct calls (15 wires), an event bus (1.03 ms and a GC every 0.9 s), a typed bus or an event queue (0.33 ms, 0 B), and catch the bugs each one brings.

  • Coupling: wires vs links
  • Boxing, list copies and garbage
  • Cascades and re-entrancy
  • Cold vs warm reaction work
  • Queues: delay, order and claiming

Services Lab

The Reef's gull needs five helpers and the score needs the save system. Get them through singletons (1 bad cold start in 25, tests take 4 minutes), a service locator (0.12 ms of lookups per step, crashes on a missing fake) or dependency injection (0 bad starts, 40 tests in 20 ms).

  • Hidden vs visible dependencies
  • Initialization order: who wakes first
  • Unit tests with fakes vs loading the scene
  • Lookups in a hot loop vs cached fields
  • Composition root and lifetimes

Gull Brain Lab

The Reef's gulls circle, dive, carry, eat, rest and flee, then shelter from storms and get stunned. Build their brain from flags (46 stuck gulls), a flat state machine (27 arrows), a hierarchical one (13 arrows) or a behavior tree (1.21 ms, then 0.61 ms).

  • Impossible flag combinations
  • States, transitions, enter and exit
  • Hierarchical states: interrupts written once
  • Behavior trees: selector, sequence, running

How labs grow with the course

Not every lesson needs a new lab. When a later lesson goes deeper into the same idea, it adds a new mode and new steps to an existing lab. That keeps the labs small and lets you compare old and new ideas side by side.
