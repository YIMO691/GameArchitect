Lesson 072: estimating technical risk

    What this shows

    A single number hides the risk

    "Six weeks" and "sum of the likely values" both assume nothing goes wrong. Unknown work has a long tail on the late side.

    Ranges make risk visible

    Best, likely and worst per part give a promise with a confidence. Wide ranges point straight at the parts to investigate.

    Spikes buy information

    A short, focused experiment on the riskiest unknown turns a 1–10 week guess into a 2–4 week plan, before the date is promised.

    This lab is a model, not a measurement. It uses Lesson 072's numbers (weeks, best / likely / worst): matchmaking 1.5 / 2 / 3, account linking 1 / 2 / 4, input fairness 0.5 / 1 / 2, cross-platform determinism 1 / 2 / 10, cross-play certification rules 0.5 / 1 / 6. After a 1-week spike: determinism 2 / 3 / 4, certification 1 / 2 / 3. Totals use the PERT estimate (best + 4 × likely + worst) ÷ 6 per part, with spread (worst − best) ÷ 6, and an 80% promise at mean + 0.84 × spread. Delivered: 15 weeks when the unknowns are found at integration (7 and 3 weeks, plus 2 weeks of rework), 13 when they're done first, 11 with a spike.
